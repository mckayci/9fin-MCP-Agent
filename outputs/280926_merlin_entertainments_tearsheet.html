Key messages on the September 2026 refinancing

1. The transaction. On 10-Sep-26 Merlin signed commitment documentation for a GBP 657m-equivalent senior secured term loan (USD 396m + EUR 418m) led by Monarch and GoldenTree, to refinance the c.GBP 630m 2027 SUNs (USD 410m 6.625% / EUR 370m 4.5%, issued by Motion Bondco DAC). Reported pricing is S/E+650bps (9fin sources), with a six-year tenor that springs to before 2030 if other debt is not refinanced. Security is shared with the existing secured debt, plus exclusive security over previously unencumbered UK attractions: the London Eye, SEA LIFE London, Shrek's Adventure! London and the UK Dungeons. Closing is expected by end-2026.

2. PF capital structure. Gross secured debt rises from GBP 3,240m (5.7x) to c.GBP 3,897m (6.9x). Total net debt is broadly unchanged at c.GBP 5.44bn (c.9.6x on GBP 568m LTM EBITDA). Unsecured debt at Bondco is eliminated. On 9fin's estimate, the deal adds c.GBP 30m of annual interest.

3. What it means for the SSNs and TLBs that were not refinanced. They gain maturity relief but lose collateral quality. A further GBP 657m of secured claims now ranks alongside them on the shared collateral (+20% of claims), and a new asset pool sits outside their reach. On a CMCK reading of the indenture, the SSNs' super senior basket could also have allowed some priority on shared collateral (unconfirmed). The market has repriced accordingly: the USD SSNs are down 12-13pts since 9-Sep, to c.63 (c.18.6-19.2% approx. YTM).

Business Description

Merlin is the world's third-largest location-based family entertainment operator by visitors: 131 attractions in 22 countries, 60.5m guests and GBP 1,999m of revenue in FY2025. It runs three brand groups: LEGOLAND Parks (11 resorts incl. Legoland Shanghai; c.43% of revenue), Gateway / Midway Attractions (indoor city-centre sites such as Madame Tussauds, SEA LIFE, the Dungeons and the London Eye; c.36%) and Resort Theme Parks (Alton Towers, Thorpe Park, Chessington, Warwick Castle, Gardaland, Heide Park; c.21%). The brand-group split is as at LTM Sep-24 (9fin Credit QuickTake, 27-Jan-25). About 82% of revenue comes from admissions and in-park spend, and c.14% from accommodation. Merlin holds the exclusive worldwide LEGOLAND licence from KIRKBI to at least 2075. From FY26 it reports three regions: North America, UK and International.

Corporate history: Merlin was taken private in Oct-2019 by KIRKBI and Blackstone (co-investors since 2005), joined by CPP Investments, at an EV of c.GBP 5.9bn (c.12x EBITDA). The deal was funded with GBP 3.3bn of new debt, lifting leverage from c.4x to c.7.5x (S&P). In FY25 Merlin took a GBP 262m impairment on the Madame Tussauds brand. It then sold its 29 LEGO / Legoland Discovery Centres to sister company The Lego Group, completing on 27-Feb-26; the net proceeds of GBP 216m were used to repay RCF drawings. Property note: per 9fin's 19-Jun-26 analysis, the new collateral sites sit on leased land, as do Alton Towers, Thorpe Park and Warwick Castle (leased from LondonMetric). The new security is therefore over operating assets and leasehold interests, not freehold real estate.

Situation Overview

Trading has diverged by region. North America is recovering: H1-26 revenue grew 5.9% at constant currency and EBITDA 20.6%, helped by the LEGO Galaxy openings at Legoland California and Florida and by cost savings. The UK has deteriorated: H1-26 revenue fell 5.8% and EBITDA 26.2%, with segment margin down from 26.0% to 20.4%. Management cited weak consumer confidence, a hot Q2, softer inbound tourism to the London Gateway sites and annual-pass revenue timing. The Middle East conflict also weighed on international travel in Q1-26. At group level, LTM revenue has fallen from GBP 2,125m (FY23) to GBP 1,938m and Adj. EBITDA from GBP 675m to GBP 568m. Net leverage has risen from 7.7x to 9.5x over the same period (S&P cut the CFR to CCC+ in Aug-25).

The capital structure has been unable to self-fund. LFCF has been negative in every period since FY23, running at c.GBP -100m to -125m a year, because cash interest (GBP 349m LTM) exceeds unlevered FCF (GBP 290m LTM). The 2027 SUNs were the pressure point: 9fin disclosed (9-Jun-26) that the RCF, TLBs and SSNs become current if the SUNs are not refinanced by 15-Jul-27 (RCF) or 15-Aug-27 (TLBs / SSNs). Merlin hired Kirkland (Jun-26) and PJT (Aug-26), considered sale-and-leasebacks, a UK aquarium sale, a new HY bond, a TLB extension and a partial sponsor equity injection, and chose secured private credit. 9fin's 10-Sep-26 analysis calls the deal a temporary fix: mid-term risk remains, with the 2029-2032 secured stack coming into focus in 2028 against c.10x leverage. Buyside sources quoted by 9fin describe it as a "band-aid". The shareholders issued a joint statement of support, but no new equity was contributed.

What the Deal Means for the SSNs and TLBs That Were Not Refinanced

The EUR 700m 2030s, USD 500m 2031s and USD 410m 2032s (SSNs), and the EUR 1,020m / USD 1,370m TLBs due Nov-29, were not refinanced. Together that is GBP 3.2bn of secured debt. The net effect is better near-term default risk, but worse recovery.

Key Catalysts

Liquidity

GBP 466m at 30-Jun-26: GBP 100m cash plus GBP 388m undrawn RCF, less GBP 22m of LCs (9fin cap table). This compares with GBP 476m at Q1-26. RCF drawings fell from GBP 160m at FY25 to GBP 40m after the LDC proceeds. Against PF LFCF of c.GBP -134m a year, headroom covers c.3.5 years of burn before seasonality (CMCK arithmetic). The PF liquidity at close is not available.

Upcoming Maturities

Nov-27: GBP 630m SUNs, to be repaid on close (expected by end-2026). May-29: GBP 428m RCF. Nov-29: TLBs, GBP 1,908m. Jun-30 / Feb-31 / Feb-32: SSNs, GBP 1,292m. c.2032: the new facility (springs to <2030). The 2029 TLBs need addressing during 2028. That is where the next wall sits, against c.10x leverage.

Covenant Headroom / Tests

RCF springing covenant: Consolidated Senior Secured Debt Ratio of 10.0x or below, tested only when the RCF is more than 40% drawn (above GBP 171m). At 30-Jun-26 the RCF was 9.3% drawn (GBP 40m) and net secured leverage was 5.5x, so the test is not live. Even PF (c.6.7x net secured) there is c.3.3x of headroom. Springing maturity: 15-Jul-27 / 15-Aug-27 if the SUNs are not refinanced; this deal is designed to remove it. Incurrence: 4.75x secured ratio debt capacity is exhausted at 5.7x, so further secured debt depends on fixed baskets.

Recommendation

Add to restructuring watchlist: Yes. The September refinancing resolves the near-term event but not the balance sheet.

1. Leverage / maturity wall: active (primary). Net leverage is c.9.6x PF, or c.10.2x on 9fin's ex-LDC EBITDA, and has not fallen since FY24. LFCF is negative, at c.GBP -134m a year PF. The next wall is GBP 1.9bn of TLBs in Nov-29, followed by GBP 1.3bn of SSNs in 2030-32, and has to be addressed during 2028. Fixed secured debt capacity has now been largely used on the new facility, and secured paper trades at 63-70. On current trajectory, a par refinancing of the remaining stack does not look available without deleveraging or new equity.

2. Liquidity: not active; approaching over 2027-28 (secondary). GBP 466m of liquidity against c.GBP 134m of annual burn gives time but no margin for further UK weakness. The PF cash position at close is not yet disclosed.

3. Covenant trip: not active. The RCF springing test (10.0x net secured, only above 40% drawn) is far from live at 9% drawn and c.6.7x PF net secured.

For creditors in the existing secured stack, the deal trades an avoided 2027 default for a larger, more senior-leaning creditor group ahead of or alongside them. The open diligence points that would change the recovery picture are:

  • the facility agreement and intercreditor (confirmed ranking, springing trigger, whether the PCL super senior basket was used)
