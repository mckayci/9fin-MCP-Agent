Business Description

Punch Pubs (formerly Punch Taverns) is the UK's seventh-largest pub operator, comprising 1,310 pubs across England, Scotland and Wales as at 17-May-2026. The estate is c.92% freehold or long-leasehold (>50 years remaining) and drink-led (c.77% of revenue), skewed to suburban/community locations (81% of the estate) rather than the high street or late-night market. Punch runs no directly-managed pubs; it operates two asset-light models through independent, largely live-in publicans: Leased & Tenanted (L&T) — 900 pubs (69% of the estate) — where Punch leases the site to a tenant and earns rent plus "tied" drink sales (c.97% of L&T pubs operate under tie agreements: below-market rent in exchange for the tenant buying most drinks from Punch at above-market prices); and Pub Partnership (PP) — 410 pubs (31%) — where Punch retains all sales and costs and pays the publican a percentage fee. PP generates higher revenue and EBITDA per site but shifts the maintenance/capex burden onto Punch; management has been actively converting L&T sites to PP (60 conversions in the 40 weeks to 17-May-26 vs. 22 a year earlier).

Corporate history: Punch Taverns underwent a three-year restructuring (2011–2014) that ended in a debt-for-equity swap cutting debt by £600m, centred on unwinding its whole-business securitisation (WBS) — a structure peers Greene King, Mitchells & Butlers and Stonegate remain tied into today. Punch exited the WBS in 2021, and in December 2021 Fortress Investment Group acquired 100% of the equity from Patron Capital for an enterprise value of c.£1.0bn (property then valued at c.£850–879m, c.93% freehold/long-leasehold). Since acquisition: in June 2025 Punch refinanced its £600m 2026 notes into £640m 7.875% Senior Secured Notes due Dec-2030 (upsizing the RCF from £70m to £85m); in February 2026 it placed a further £50m fungible tap of the same notes at a 102.75% premium, funding bolt-on freehold pub acquisitions rather than plugging a liquidity gap.

Freehold property — the valuation floor behind the credit

Punch's freehold/long-leasehold estate (92% of sites) is independently valued by Savills at £1,079.4m (17-May-2026 cap table date), up from £992.0m (Feb-2025) and £849.7m (2021). Against net debt of £749.3m (incl. leases), that implies asset coverage of ~1.44x — roughly £330m of headroom over and above the going-concern EBITDA-multiple story, and the basis for the 62.8% LTV the company itself reports (in preference to a pure EBITDA multiple) as its primary leverage lens. Security is a fixed first equitable charge over freehold and most leasehold real estate — lighter-touch than a fully perfected legal mortgage, with no requirement to register/perfect unless acceleration occurs. The Savills valuation is also a going-concern, largely desktop-style exercise (a 2021-era 9fin risk-factor extract on this estate noted "limited due diligence and property inspections"), so treat £1.1bn as a useful reference floor, not a guaranteed recovery number in a genuine distressed scenario (illiquidity, use-restriction covenants and a possible wave of independent-pub disposals could compress realisable value). Freehold ownership also gives Punch demonstrated disposal optionality that leaseholders lack: £92.6m of net proceeds from 172 pub disposals (2020–Feb-2025) at a 21.4x EBITDA multiple, though that pace has slowed sharply in FY26 (£3.1m/6 pubs vs. £8.4m/19 pubs a year earlier) as management pivots to net freehold acquisitions instead.

Situation Overview

Trading has improved consistently through FY26. Revenue for the 40 weeks to 17-May-26 was £274.7m (+9.1% YoY) and underlying EBITDA was £75.9m (+6.6% YoY); the subsequent Q3 FY26 earnings flash (to end-July) showed sales +8.6% YoY and EBITDA +5.8% YoY, with net leverage improving 0.1x to 6.6x on a secured basis (7.3x on an IFRS16 basis). Growth has been driven by like-for-like price/volume gains, continued L&T-to-PP conversion, and bolt-on freehold acquisitions (50 pubs for £36.2m in the period, funded by the February 2026 tap). Management described Q4 trading (to 12-Jul-26) as strong, helped by elevated England World Cup viewing (PP like-for-like sales +59% across the seven England match days).

One nuance worth flagging against the prior "Pass" call: LTM free cash flow has deteriorated sharply, and levered FCF has turned negative (£-6.6m), not because trading is weakening — underlying EBITDA is at a series high (£103.1m LTM) — but because net capex has nearly doubled (£27.4m FY25 to £49.8m LTM) on the back of the accelerated L&T-to-PP conversion programme and the freehold bolt-on acquisitions, compounded by higher cash interest on the enlarged £690m note balance. UFCF/EBITDA conversion fell from 77.3% (FY25) to 50.2% (LTM); LFCF/EBITDA is n.a. (negative). This is capex-funded, not EBITDA-driven, deterioration — see Summary Financials, p.3.

The one credit-relevant management change since the estate was last screened (27-Sep-2026) is at CFO level: Matt Ward (ex-CFO, Village Hotels; prior senior finance roles at Greene King) took over as CFO on 11-May-26, with 15-year incumbent Steve Dando moving into a newly created Chief Financial Strategist role rather than departing — a retention-oriented transition, not a distress signal. Near-term outlook per management is continued benefit from like-for-like growth, PP conversions, bolt-on M&A and cost optimisation; no mention of refinancing pressure, covenant concern or trading deterioration.

Note on leverage bases: 9fin's cap table shows 7.27x net total leverage (incl. £71.9m of IFRS16 leases) off £103.1m underlying EBITDA; the company's own Q3 FY26 guidance is 6.6x net (secured basis), against a stated financial policy target of below 6.0x. These are calculated on different EBITDA/debt bases (the company measure appears to net out leases and/or use a run-rate EBITDA including acquisition benefit) and are shown separately here, not blended — see Capital Structure, p.2.

Key Catalysts

Liquidity

£97.6m as at 17-May-2026 (£12.6m cash + £85.0m fully undrawn RCF), up from £94.0m at FY25 year-end. Operating cash flow for the 40-week period was £64.1m — no indication of cash burn. Full £85m of RCF headroom is available.

Upcoming Maturities

£690m 7.875% Senior Secured Notes due 30-Dec-2030 (c.51 months out). £85m Super Senior RCF due Jun-2030, fully undrawn. No maturity wall within the standard 18–24 month screening window.

Covenant Headroom / Tests

Actual secured net leverage (6.6x) currently sits marginally above the notes' 6.5x senior secured net leverage incurrence gate — a capacity constraint on further ratio debt/restricted payments, not a maintenance test and not a breach. The RCF carries a springing covenant capping net cash RCF drawings' LTV at 0.15x, tested quarterly only once drawings exceed 40% of the £85m facility (£34.0m); with the RCF fully undrawn (0%), this test is not currently live. No portfolio-wide maintenance LTV covenant exists on the notes despite the prominence of the property-value disclosure.

Sector Context — is this a Punch problem or a pub-sector problem?

A creditor recently characterised UK pubs as "struggling." On the evidence gathered, this is best read as a sector-wide narrative driven primarily by Stonegate (TDR Capital) and by macro cost pressures, not a Punch-specific signal. Stonegate's Q3 FY26 (18-Sep-26) showed sales -8.6% YoY, net leverage still 8.1x (vs. Punch's 6.6x), a c.88% lease-adjusted LTV (vs. Punch's 62.8%), a CCC+ Fitch rating (vs. Punch's B3/B-), a whole-business-securitisation-plus-unrestricted-subsidiary structure 9fin's own legal team has flagged as complex, and reported talks (23-Sep-26) to sell hundreds of pubs to Heineken "as debts mount." Separately, there is a genuine sector-wide cost headwind — National Living Wage +4.1% and employer NIC +1.2pt through 2025–26 (c.£2,500/FTE for operators bearing labour directly), nearly two pub closures a day nationally in Q1 2026, and an HM Treasury business-rates review announced August 2026 — but this bites hardest on managed/food-led operators who directly employ staff, not on Punch, whose independently-operated L&T/PP model pushes labour costs onto tenants and partnership operators.

Freehold ownership is the clearest structural differentiator. Punch is the most freehold-heavy of the UK's four major pubcos (92%), giving it a materially lower LTV, demonstrated disposal optionality, and an asset base (Savills £1,079.4m) that over-collateralises the £690m of secured notes by c.1.44x. Stonegate, by contrast, carries c.88% lease-adjusted LTV and carved its own freehold Platinum estate (944 tied + 90 free-of-tie pubs) into an unrestricted subsidiary in 2024 — reducing, not adding to, creditor recourse to that freehold value. 9fin's own September 2026 distress tracker adds five names this month (Awaze, Iberconsa, Hilding Anders, Landal, Wagamama) but does not add Punch or Stonegate, though Stonegate's weaker metrics mean it may already sit on an earlier month's list (out of scope for this pull).

Recommendation

Add to restructuring watchlist: No. None of the three key triggers is present or clearly approaching for Punch specifically:

1. Covenant trip — not active. Actual leverage (6.6x) sits marginally above the notes' 6.5x incurrence gate, restricting further ratio debt/restricted payments, but this is a capacity constraint, not a maintenance breach. The RCF's springing LTV covenant is untested (0% drawn vs. the 40%-of-commitments activation threshold).

2. Leverage / maturity wall — not active. The £690m notes mature Dec-2030 (c.51 months out), well outside the 18–24 month screening window, and Punch has demonstrated continued capital-markets access on favourable terms (the Feb-2026 tap priced at a premium to fund accretive M&A, not to plug a gap).

3. Liquidity — not active, and improving. £97.6m available, RCF fully undrawn, up from £94.0m at FY25 year-end. The one genuine watch item is the LFCF turning negative on the capex step-up (see p.3) — worth tracking, but capex-funded rather than trading-driven, and the group retains full disposal optionality against a £1.1bn freehold base if a liquidity need ever arose.

On the specific question of property: freehold ownership is Punch's single clearest source of resilience relative to peers — it lowers LTV, funds an inflation hedge (c.a third of EBITDA is RPI/CPI-linked rental income), provides disposal optionality, and over-collateralises the secured notes by c.1.44x on an independent (if desktop-style) valuation. It cuts both ways operationally: as Punch converts sites from L&T to the higher-margin PP model, it takes on the maintenance capex that tenants previously bore, which is the direct cause of the FCF deterioration flagged above. Recommendation: maintain Pass, but monitor sector cost inflation (NLW/NIC/business-rates review outcome) and Stonegate's Heineken process as a leading indicator for the wider UK pub sub-sector, given Punch's comparative resilience could still be tested if cost pressure or consumer discretionary spend deteriorates further.
