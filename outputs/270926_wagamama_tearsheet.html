Business Description

Wagamama is the UK's largest pan-Asian casual dining chain, operating a single "wagamama" brand across noodle- and rice-based dishes, small plates and drinks, positioned as an affordable, health-focused alternative within UK casual dining. The first restaurant opened in Bloomsbury, London in 1992. As at FY2025 year-end, the estate comprised 166 owned UK & Ireland restaurants, six owned US sites, and 54 franchised sites (primarily Europe and the Middle East), plus an early-stage 50%-owned India joint venture.

Revenue mix: 95–98% of revenue is generated by UK & Ireland company-operated restaurants. Dine-in remains the largest channel; delivery (Deliveroo since 2016, Uber Eats added Q2 2026) represents c.19–21% of UK sales, guided by management to reach c.25% on a run-rate basis by end-2026. Average main-meal price was £15.49 (FY2024), reflecting a deliberate value/volume positioning. Under IFRS 8 the group discloses one reportable operating segment.

Corporate history: The Restaurant Group plc (TRG) acquired Wagamama in 2018 at c.12.2x EBITDA, making it TRG's largest brand within a wider multi-brand estate (Frankie & Benny's, Chiquito, Brunning & Price, TRG Concessions) that was progressively rationalised through the following years. Apollo Global Management completed a take-private of TRG on 22-Dec-2023 (c.£701m EV, c.9x EBITDA). In January 2025, as part of a refinancing, Apollo split TRG into three standalone, separately-financed divisions — Wagamama, Pubs (Brunning & Price) and Concessions — each sitting under Rock BidCo Limited. Wagamama's restricted group issued £330m of 8.5% Senior Secured Notes due 2030 to refinance a prior sterling unitranche facility. In July 2026, TRG sold its Concessions division (38 airport sites, c.€240m revenue) to Areas Group; Wagamama continues to trade in UK airports as a franchisee of Areas.

The Wagamama / TRG perimeter

The £330m SSNs and £55m RCF sit in a Wagamama-only financing silo (issuer: Waga BondCo Limited; guarantors: Wagamama (Holdings) Limited and Wagamama Limited), legally and structurally separate from TRG's Pubs and Concessions silos — confirmed independently by both 9fin's org chart/OM and S&P's recovery analysis. Apollo owns all three silos in parallel via Rock BidCo (~98.1%), not through a TRG-controlled chain sitting over Wagamama. That said, operational integration persists: Mark Chambers is CFO of TRG and CEO of Wagamama, both he and TRG CEO Andy Hornby present under TRG titles on bondholder calls, and TRG Holdings continues to recharge Wagamama for shared services (c.£9–12m/year) under an uncapped restricted-payments basket — a value-leakage risk flagged by 9fin's legal analysis. No shareholder loan/PIK is currently on file above the restricted group, but Waga Topco and Waga Midco sit structurally outside it, which is latent (unconfirmed) headroom for one to sit there invisibly to reported leverage. See Group Structure (p.2) for the full chart.

Situation Overview

2025 was a weak trading year: S&P-adjusted EBITDA fell to £79.6m (16.6% margin) from £88.5m in 2024, and revenue contracted 4.2% to £480.5m, partly reflecting the transfer of four airport sites into the TRG Concessions business ahead of its eventual sale. 9fin's November 2025 analysis attributed the pressure to a "bleak UK economic environment": food inflation of 5.6% (Oct-2025), weak consumer confidence, and higher labour costs from the October 2024 Budget's employer NIC and national living wage increases. Management deliberately held pricing flat through 2025 to protect volumes, which compressed margins further.

H1 FY26 results (reported 8-Sep-2026) showed sales +4.7% YoY but EBITDA -23.5% YoY, driven by the annualising cost of value-reset initiatives and the final quarter of NIC increases; net leverage rose 0.3x to 5.2x (pre-IFRS16, PF basis). Management described the business as having "reached an inflection point," citing dine-in volume growth (+3.4% in Q2), a new Uber Eats delivery partnership (delivery like-for-like +21.6% since rollout), and guided to a return to EBITDA growth in Q3 26, strengthening further in Q4 26. This is broadly consistent with, though slightly more optimistic than, S&P's base case of 3–4% revenue growth and margin recovery to 17.0–17.5% in 2026.

The main deleveraging action to date is the January 2025 refinancing and corporate split itself, which ended the prior TRG intercompany cash-sweep arrangement (previously priced at SONIA+800bps and understating Wagamama's true standalone interest cost). Following the Concessions sale, management flagged on the Q2 26 call a "further review of group central costs," with the TRG head-office recharge expected to reduce into FY27.

Both 9fin and S&P expect 2026 to remain a transition year with recovery weighted to H2. S&P forecasts lease-inclusive leverage of 6.2–6.5x in 2026 (from 6.9x in 2025), improving to 5.7–6.1x in 2027, with free operating cash flow after leases staying negative (£6–10m outflow) in 2026 before turning roughly neutral in 2027 — the primary basis for S&P's negative outlook (25-Jun-2026).

Note on leverage bases: figures quoted across sources are not directly comparable. The screen/9fin unadjusted pre-IFRS16 basis gives 7.5x; the company's own PF-adjusted basis (which governs the RCF covenant) gives 5.19x net; S&P's lease-inclusive adjusted basis gives 6.9x (FY25) easing to 6.2–6.5x (FY26e). Do not blend these — see Summary Financials (p.3) for full detail.

Key Catalysts

Liquidity

£43.7m as at 30-Jun-2026 (£3.9m cash + £39.8m undrawn RCF; £15.2m of £55m drawn, 27.6%). S&P's 31-Mar-2026 snapshot showed materially stronger liquidity (£8.9m cash, £52.5m RCF availability) — a real deterioration of c.£5m cash and c.£12.7m of incremental RCF drawdown within one quarter, predating S&P's "adequate" call. S&P flags this adequacy as explicitly conditional on FOCF after leases improving from -£20.8m (FY25) to the -£6m to -£10m guided for FY26.

Upcoming Maturities

£330m 8.5% SSNs due 15-Jun-2030 (c.45 months out); £55m super senior RCF due Jan-2030. No maturity wall within the standard 18–24 month screening window — the covenant and liquidity triggers below are the near-term watch items.

Covenant Headroom / Tests

The RCF carries a springing net leverage covenant of 8.3x, tested quarterly only once drawings exceed 40% of the £55m facility (£22.0m). At 30-Jun-2026 drawings stood at £15.2m (27.6%) — approximately £6.8m of further drawdown would cross the threshold and activate testing. If tested, headroom appears wide on the covenant-relevant company basis (5.19x actual vs. 8.3x trigger); S&P separately estimates "headroom of at least 20%" over the next 12 months. Neither 9fin nor S&P currently expects an actual breach — the risk being monitored is test activation, not failure, given negative FOCF.

Recommendation

Add to restructuring watchlist: Yes — Monitor, not escalate. None of the three key triggers is currently active, but two warrant active quarterly monitoring:

1. Covenant trip — approaching, not active. The RCF's 8.3x springing covenant is untested (27.6% drawn vs. the 40% activation threshold), and neither 9fin nor S&P expects an actual breach if tested (S&P estimates at least 20% headroom). Only c.£6.8m of further drawdown would activate testing, and management has confirmed the RCF will carry a year-end balance in FY26 due to working-capital seasonality — a live, quarterly-monitorable item.

2. Leverage / maturity wall — not currently active. The £330m SSNs do not mature until June 2030, well outside the 18–24 month screening window. S&P's leverage trajectory shows limited near-term deleveraging momentum (6.9x FY25 to 6.2–6.5x FY26e), so this is one to revisit as 2028–2029 refinancing planning begins, not now.

3. Liquidity — the primary live concern. Liquidity is rated "adequate" by S&P but explicitly conditional on FOCF after leases improving from -£20.8m (FY25); cash on balance sheet is thin (£3.9m), making the business structurally reliant on RCF headroom through the trading cycle. If the guided H2 26 EBITDA recovery does not materialise, liquidity is the trigger most likely to deteriorate first, and would also pull the covenant test into range.

On the TRG/Wagamama perimeter specifically: no evidence of further corporate reorganisation or re-consolidation of Wagamama's reporting into TRG since the January 2025 split was found in 9fin's document set, the S&P report, or the Q2 26 bondholder transcript — the legal Restricted Group remains confined to Wagamama. Operational and management integration with TRG persists (shared CEO/CFO titles, ongoing TRG head-office recharges under an uncapped basket), and 9fin's Jan-2025 legal analysis on value leakage and structural subordination risk has not been superseded by anything found in this review. Both remain live structural considerations for bondholders even though the operating credit story is separable from TRG's other divisions. Revisit each quarter against: (i) whether the guided Q3/Q4 26 EBITDA growth materialises; (ii) RCF drawings vs. the 40% covenant threshold; (iii) any further rating action (both agencies currently negative outlook).
