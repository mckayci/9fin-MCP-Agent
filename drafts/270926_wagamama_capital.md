## 0. Perimeter Question — Wagamama vs TRG vs Apollo (answered up front)

**Ciarán's question, in short: the £330m SSNs and £55m RCF sit in a Wagamama-only financing silo (Waga BondCo Limited), fully separated — legally and structurally — from TRG's legacy/residual assets. TRG entities are outside the restricted group. Apollo owns the whole complex above the silo via BidCo, and there is no shareholder debt currently identified inside 9fin's coverage, but there is structural room for one to sit above the restricted group without appearing in reported leverage. There are real, ongoing intercompany cash leakage points (TRG service/recharge agreements) that restrict-group creditors should watch.**

Detail:

1. **Issuer/borrower entity.** The £330m 8.500% Senior Secured Notes due Jun-2030 and the £55m super senior RCF due Jan-2030 are both issued/borrowed out of **Waga BondCo Limited**, a Wagamama-specific financing entity several layers below both Apollo and TRG. They are guaranteed by **Wagamama (Holdings) Limited** (the parent guarantor and holding company of the Wagamama group post-reorganisation) and **Wagamama Limited** (the subsidiary guarantor / UK operating company) **[9fin, org chart]**. This is independently confirmed by S&P's 25-Jun-2026 Research Update **[S&P upload]**, which states explicitly in its recovery analysis: *"Waga BondCo Ltd. is the issuer of the notes, the parent guarantor is Wagamama (Holdings) Ltd., and the subsidiary guarantor is Wagamama Ltd."* S&P also confirms the £55m super senior RCF ranks ahead of the notes in the waterfall and is itself unrated. Moody's and S&P CFRs (B2/B) are assigned at Wagamama (Holdings) Limited, not at TRG or BidCo level. This is a Wagamama-specific entity, not a TRG entity and not the ultimate holdco.

2. **Is TRG / residual non-Wagamama assets inside or outside the restricted group?** Outside. Per 9fin's Credit QuickTake (Jan-2025) **[9fin]**: *"The Restricted Group is confined solely to the Wagamama[business]."* As part of the Jan-2025 refinancing, TRG split into three standalone silos sitting side by side under **Rock BidCo Limited**: (i) Wagamama (Waga Topco → Waga Midco → Wagamama (Holdings) → Waga BondCo), (ii) Pubs (Brunning & Price brand, via Pubs PledgeCo Limited → Pubs HoldCo Limited → Pubs subsidiaries), and (iii) a third TRG-legacy/Concessions chain (Rock BidCo Holdings Limited → The Restaurant Group Limited → TRG (Holdings) Ltd → C&B HoldCo Limited → C&B subsidiaries). The Pubs and Concessions/TRG-legacy silos are financed separately — 9fin reporting confirms Pubs and Concessions "are entering into separate term loans and revolving credit facilities as part of the reorganisation" — so they carry their own debt and are not cross-collateralised or cross-guaranteed with Wagamama's restricted group. None of these entities are guarantors of the £330m SSNs or the RCF.
   - **Independent evidence of separation from the S&P report [S&P upload]:** S&P notes that in 2025, "the transfer of four airport sites to the TRG concessions business depressed revenue by £25 million-£30 million," and that excluding this transfer, Wagamama's own revenue rose 1.5% to £480.5m. This confirms in a primary source, separate from the 9fin org chart, that (a) a distinct "TRG concessions business" exists outside Wagamama, matching the TRG-legacy/Concessions silo in the org chart, and (b) real operating assets (four airport sites) were actually transferred out of the Wagamama perimeter into that TRG entity during 2025 — a concrete example of the perimeter being actively managed/reshaped by the sponsor, not just a static legal structure. S&P separately notes a £2.6m FY25 cash outflow related to "turnover rent from the transfer of the airport sites," an incremental (one-off) cost the Wagamama restricted group bore in connection with that transfer.
   - Note on the org chart geometry: within the Wagamama silo itself, **Waga Topco Limited and Waga Midco Limited sit above the dashed "Restricted Group" boundary** — the restricted group (per the chart) starts at Wagamama (Holdings) Limited and runs down through Waga BondCo, Wagamama Limited and non-guarantor subsidiaries. Topco/Midco are structurally part of the "Wagamama" ownership branch but are **not** inside the covenant-defined restricted group.

3. **Where Apollo sits.** Per the Final OM **[9fin]**: certain investment funds managed by **Apollo** indirectly hold **~98.1%** of Rock BidCo's issued ordinary share capital; funds managed by **Conversion Venture Capital** hold **~1.1%**; the remainder is held indirectly by other operating partners, including members of TRG senior management. Apollo therefore sits at the top of the whole complex (BidCo and above, shown as "Principal Shareholders" in the org chart), owning both the Wagamama silo and the residual TRG/Pubs/Concessions silos in parallel — not sequentially through a TRG-branded holdco that also controls Wagamama's financing. S&P's company description **[S&P upload]** independently states plainly that "the company is owned by financial sponsor Apollo Global Management Inc.," consistent with the OM disclosure. S&P's governance modifier is "moderately negative," reflecting its general view that sponsor-owned, highly leveraged issuers are run with a focus on maximising shareholder (i.e. Apollo) returns over a finite holding period — a relevant lens on the TRG service-agreement and asset-transfer dynamics above, though S&P does not flag Wagamama specifically for this beyond the standard sponsor-ownership caveat.
   - **On shareholder loans / PIK above the restricted group:** 9fin's cap table and debt/covenants table for Wagamama show only the £55m RCF and £330m SSNs — no PIK notes or shareholder loans are currently on file. S&P's own capital structure modifier is "Neutral" and its financial policy score is "Fs-6," with no mention in the report of any shareholder loan, PIK note, or debt instrument above Waga BondCo Ltd / Wagamama (Holdings) Ltd. However, because Waga Topco Limited and Waga Midco Limited sit structurally above the restricted group boundary, there is headroom in the structure for a shareholder loan or PIK instrument to be introduced at Topco/Midco level that would **not** be captured in Wagamama's reported net leverage under any of the three bases in this note (company PF-adjusted 5.19x-5.25x, S&P-adjusted 6.2x-6.5x, or the screen's unadjusted 7.5x — see Section 1). This is a structural feature to monitor, not a confirmed instrument — **further diligence required**: check Companies House filings for Waga Topco Limited / Waga Midco Limited for any registered charges or loan notes, as neither 9fin nor the S&P report holds instrument-level data for entities outside the restricted group.

4. **Other Apollo/TRG intercompany arrangements affecting cash available to the restricted group.** Confirmed and material [9fin unless marked]:
   - **Services Agreement** — TRG Holdings (the group-level shared-services entity, also Apollo-owned) provides head-office/central functions to Wagamama on a stated arm's-length basis, charged pro-rata to Wagamama's share of total TRG group revenue. Disclosed historical charges: £9.0m (FY22), £11.9m (FY23), £11.9m (LTM to Jan-2025), £9.4m (LTM to Jun-2025); the latest 9fin cap table (30-Jun-2026) still adds back £10m of "TRG Group costs" in arriving at PF adjusted EBITDA, confirming the arrangement is ongoing.
   - **Recharge Agreement** — passes through the benefit of certain third-party contracts held at TRG/TRG Holdings level to the Wagamama group on an arm's-length basis.
   - **VAT Equalization Agreement** — intended to prevent the Waga Topco Group bearing VAT liabilities beyond what it would owe as a standalone VAT group.
   - **Value-leakage flag (9fin Legal QuickTake):** the OM's restricted payments covenant permits **uncapped** restricted payments to the HoldCo Group in respect of these transitional service arrangements. Because TRG Holdings is also Apollo-owned, 9fin flags this as a potential value-leakage channel for restricted-group creditors — the payments are represented as being for real, arm's-length services, but the absence of a cap is a monitoring point.
   - **Historical intercompany funding (pre-2025, now unwound):** while Wagamama still sat inside TRG's cash pool, intercompany balances between Wagamama and TRG were priced at SONIA+800bps; in 2024 Wagamama accrued interest income from TRG of £17.2m, TRG swept £41m of cash out of Wagamama, and Wagamama recorded £33.8m of accrued interest owed to TRG. A one-off £14.6m cash outflow was incurred at the Jan-2025 refinancing to settle these pre-transaction intercompany balances — **independently confirmed by S&P [S&P upload]**, which itemises FY25's negative £20.8m FOCF after leases as including "£14.6 million of intercompany cash sweeps and £2.6 million of outflows related to the restructuring, including bonus payouts and turnover rent from the transfer of the airport sites," and states it does "not expect these to persist given the changes in the group structure post the refinancing." Management now expects working-capital and cash management to be fully standalone following separation, but the scale of historical cash sweeps is a reminder of how much of Wagamama's cash was previously controlled at TRG level.

**Bottom line for the tearsheet:** the £330m SSNs and RCF are ring-fenced in a Wagamama-only restricted group, structurally insulated from TRG's other (separately financed) brands. Apollo controls the whole complex from above BidCo. The main perimeter risk to flag is not co-mingled debt, but recurring, uncapped-by-covenant cash payments from the restricted group to the Apollo-owned TRG Holdings shared-services entity, plus latent (though currently unconfirmed) headroom for a shareholder loan/PIK to sit at Waga Topco/Midco outside reported leverage.

**Liquidity has deteriorated materially between the two reference dates.** Comparing S&P's 31-Mar-2026 snapshot (£8.9m cash, £52.5m RCF availability implying only ~£2.5m drawn) against 9fin's 30-Jun-2026 cap table (£3.9m cash, £39.8m undrawn implying £15.2m drawn) shows cash fell by £5.0m and RCF drawings rose by ~£12.7m over the quarter — consistent with the weak Q2 2026 results (EBITDA -23.5% YoY, net leverage +0.3x to 5.2x, per 9fin's 8-Sep-2026 earnings flash). This is worth flagging explicitly: S&P's "adequate" liquidity call was made using Mar-2026 data, before the extent of the Q2 deterioration was known, and its negative outlook itself notes "our forecast for adequate liquidity could come under pressure if negative FOCF after leases does not improve."

RCF springing-covenant headroom (distinct from cash liquidity headroom): the 8.3x leverage test only activates once RCF drawings exceed 40% (£22.0m). At current (30-Jun-2026) drawings of £15.2m, there is **£6.8m of further RCF drawdown capacity before the covenant springs into effect**. Once tested, headroom to the 8.3x threshold is ~3.1x on the company's PF adjusted EBITDA basis (5.19x net leverage), ~1.8x-2.1x on S&P's adjusted basis (6.2x-6.5x FY26e), but only ~0.8x on the unadjusted LTM basis used in this week's screen (7.5x). S&P itself estimates "headroom of at least 20% under this covenant over the next 12 months" — broadly consistent with its 6.2x-6.5x forecast sitting comfortably below 8.3x. The covenant is tested using the OM's own EBITDA definition (closer to the company PF-adjusted basis), so the wider cushion is the operative one for compliance purposes, but the S&P and screen views are the more conservative lenses for assessing underlying credit deterioration.

9fin commentary (Nov-2025 Analysis piece) flagged that levered FCF was expected to turn negative in FY25 post-refinancing given ~£25-26m of cash interest, with a modelled ~£15m RCF drawdown to fund cash burn and maintain a ~£10m cash buffer. S&P's actual reported cash interest for FY25 was higher, at £40.8m (reflecting one-off refinancing costs), and FY25 FOCF after leases came in at -£20.8m (including the £14.6m intercompany sweep settlement and £2.6m restructuring-related outflows discussed in Section 0). S&P forecasts FOCF after leases of -£10m to -£6m in FY26 and roughly neutral (-£4m to +£1.0m) in FY27 — liquidity is expected to stay under pressure through FY26 before stabilising.
