## Situation Overview

Wagamama is the UK's largest pan-Asian casual dining chain (166 owned UK sites, six owned US sites, 54 franchised sites internationally). It became the principal remaining trading asset of The Restaurant Group (TRG) following Apollo's December 2023 take-private of TRG and a January 2025 corporate reorganisation that split TRG into three standalone-financed divisions — Wagamama, Pubs (Brunning & Price) and Concessions — each ring-fenced with its own debt (9fin, *Wagamama — Credit QuickTake*, 20-Jan-2025; 9fin, *How spicy is Wagamama's new bond deal?*, 20-Jan-2025). Wagamama refinanced its unitranche facility with a £330m 8.5% Senior Secured Notes issue due June 2030 alongside a £55m super senior RCF due January 2030.

**Trading performance.** 2025 was a weak year across the group: S&P-adjusted EBITDA fell to £79.6m (16.6% margin) from £88.5m in 2024, and revenue contracted 4.2% to £480.5m, partly reflecting the transfer of four airport sites into the TRG Concessions business (S&P Report, 25-Jun-2026, p.1). 9fin's November 2025 analysis attributed the pressure to a "bleak UK economic environment" — food inflation of 5.6% (Oct-2025), weak consumer confidence, and higher labour costs from the October 2024 Budget's employer NIC and national living wage increases (9fin, *Wagamama, turning noodles into ramen*, 28-Nov-2025). Management deliberately held pricing flat through 2025 to protect volumes rather than pass on costs, which compressed margins further.

H1 FY26 results (reported 8-Sep-2026) showed continued margin pressure: sales +4.7% YoY, but EBITDA -23.5% YoY, driven by the annualising cost of the value-reset initiatives and the final quarter of NIC increases. Net leverage rose 0.3x to 5.2x (pre-IFRS16 basis) in the period (9fin, Q2 Earnings Flash, 8-Sep-2026). Management (Andy Hornby, CEO of TRG, and Mark Chambers, CFO of TRG and CEO of Wagamama) described the business as having "reached an inflection point," citing positive dine-in volume growth (+3.4% in Q2 26), successful expansion of delivery via a new Uber Eats partnership (delivery like-for-like +21.6% since rollout) alongside the existing Deliveroo relationship, and guided to a return to EBITDA growth in Q3 26 and further strengthening in Q4 26 as the prior year's cost investment annualises (Wagamama Q2 2026 Bondholder Transcript, 8-Sep-2026). This guidance is consistent with, though slightly more optimistic in tone than, S&P's own base case of 3-4% revenue growth and EBITDA margin recovery to 17.0-17.5% in 2026 (S&P Report, p.2-3).

**Deleveraging / restructuring actions.** The main structural action taken was the January 2025 refinancing and corporate split itself, which separated Wagamama's capital structure from TRG's Pubs and Concessions divisions and ended the prior intercompany cash-sweep arrangement with TRG (which had been priced at SONIA+800bps and materially understated Wagamama's true standalone interest cost). TRG completed the sale of its Concessions division to Areas Group in July 2026 (c. €240m revenue, 38 airport sites, ~2,200 staff), with Wagamama continuing to be operated in UK airports by Areas under a franchise agreement (Restaurant Online, 16-Jul-2026; Hospitality & Catering News, Jul-2026). On the Q2 26 bondholder call, management flagged a "further review of group central costs" following the Concessions disposal, with the TRG head-office cost recharge to Wagamama (currently allocated on a revenue basis, £9.4m in the LTM to Jun-2025) expected to reduce into FY27 as the transitional services arrangement with the former Concessions business rolls off.

**Near-term outlook.** Both 9fin and S&P expect 2026 to remain a transition year with a return to growth weighted to H2. S&P forecasts leverage (its own adjusted basis, including lease liabilities) of 6.2x-6.5x in 2026 (from 6.9x in 2025), improving to 5.7x-6.1x in 2027, with free operating cash flow after leases staying negative (£6m-£10m outflow) in 2026 before turning roughly neutral in 2027 (S&P Report, p.2-3). This is the primary basis for S&P's negative outlook, issued 25-Jun-2026. 9fin's November 2025 analysis was more constructive, expecting Wagamama to "meet its liquidity needs from 2027 onward" as one-off refinancing costs unwind and new-site openings lift EBITDA, while explicitly not expecting a breach of the RCF's 8.3x springing leverage covenant.

*Note on leverage bases: figures quoted across sources are not always like-for-like. 9fin's cap table (30-Jun-2026) shows net leverage of 5.19x on a pre-IFRS16 basis (net debt £341.3m / PF adjusted EBITDA £65.7m, excluding £200.1m of lease liabilities). S&P's leverage of 6.9x (2025) / 6.2x-6.5x (2026e) is calculated on an S&P-adjusted basis that includes lease liabilities as debt. Both are legitimate but not directly comparable; this tearsheet flags the basis wherever a figure is quoted.*

## Key Catalysts

**Liquidity.**
- Reported liquidity of £43.7m as at 30-Jun-2026: £3.9m cash plus £39.8m undrawn RCF (9fin cap table, 30-Jun-2026). RCF drawn £15.2m of £55m (27.6% drawn).
- S&P separately assesses liquidity as "adequate," with sources expected to cover uses by more than 1.2x over the 12 months from 31-Mar-2026 (S&P Report, p.4). S&P's liquidity sources include £8.9m cash, £52.5m RCF availability and £20m-£25m of cash FFO; uses include £25m-£30m capex, up to £5m of working-capital outflow and c.£10m of seasonal working-capital swings.
- S&P flags this adequacy assessment as conditional: "our forecast for adequate liquidity could come under pressure if negative FOCF after leases does not improve" (S&P Report, p.1). FOCF after leases was -£20.8m in 2025 and is forecast at -£6m to -£10m in 2026, turning roughly neutral (-£4m to +£1m) only in 2027.
- Management confirmed on the Q2 26 bondholder call that the RCF will remain drawn at year-end FY26 because of a working-capital outflow tied to the 53-week accounting year, and gave FY26 guidance of c.£28m cash interest, c.£28m-£30m capex, and c.£3m-£3.5m cash tax.

**Upcoming maturities.**
- £330m 8.5% Senior Secured Notes (Waga BondCo Ltd), maturing 15-Jun-2030 — c.45 months from this update. No near-term refinancing requirement, but S&P's recovery analysis (year-of-default scenario 2029) and elevated leverage trajectory mean refinancing terms at 2030 will depend on the trajectory of deleveraging over the next 2-3 years.
- £55m super senior RCF, maturing Jan-2030.
- No maturity wall within the standard 18-24 month screening window; the covenant/liquidity triggers are the more immediate concerns.

**Covenant headroom / tests.**
- The RCF carries a springing net leverage covenant of 8.3x (consolidated senior secured debt ratio), tested quarterly only once RCF drawings (net of cash) exceed 40% of total RCF commitments (£22m of the £55m facility).
- As at 30-Jun-2026, drawings stood at £15.2m (27.6% drawn) — approximately £6.8m of further net drawdown would cross the 40% threshold and activate testing.
- If tested, actual headroom against the 8.3x trigger appears wide on both available bases: 9fin's pre-IFRS16 net leverage was 5.19x at 30-Jun-2026, and S&P "expect[s] headroom of at least 20% under this covenant over the next 12 months" (i.e. actual ratio no higher than c.6.6x against the 8.3x trigger) (S&P Report, p.5). Neither 9fin's November 2025 analysis nor the S&P report anticipates an actual breach; the risk is the test being activated (via a covenant-basis leverage figure that is not identical to the reported net leverage figures above) rather than being failed, though this should be monitored each quarter given negative FOCF.

## 9fin Analysis Integration

1. **Wagamama — Credit QuickTake (9fin, 20-Jan-2025)** and **Bond Legal QuickTake (9fin, 20-Jan-2025):** Set out the mechanics of the TRG corporate reorganisation. The Restricted Group for the notes is confined solely to Wagamama; TRG Holdings (also Apollo-owned) provides services to Wagamama under an arm's-length Transitional Services Agreement that permits **uncapped** restricted payments to the HoldCo Group, which 9fin flagged as a **value leakage risk** for Wagamama bondholders. The legal analysis separately flagged a heightened **structural subordination** risk: there is no non-guarantor debt sub-limit for Ratio Debt, meaning the Issuer can transfer assets (including collateral) to non-guarantor Restricted Subsidiaries without a cap.
2. **How spicy is Wagamama's new bond deal? (9fin, 20-Jan-2025):** Market sources described the TRG split as consistent with Apollo's original 2023 thesis of separating three distinct businesses for eventual individual sale; a buysider view was that Wagamama, as the "highest quality asset" in the former TRG portfolio, was better issued standalone than blended with weaker brands (e.g. former Frankie & Benny's).
3. **Wagamama, turning noodles into ramen — Analysis (9fin, 28-Nov-2025):** 9fin's central credit view going into FY26 — expects a further difficult 2025/26 but a gradual recovery from 2027, driven by continued (if slower) site expansion, with FCF headroom limited but the 8.3x springing covenant unlikely to be breached (Q2 25 net leverage of 4.4x cited at the time). This piece also confirmed that pre-2025 working-capital dynamics were distorted by TRG-level intercompany cash pooling (accrued interest owed to TRG of £33.8m and cash sweeps of £41m in FY24 alone), and that these imbalances are expected to normalise now that Wagamama operates outside the TRG cash pool.
4. **S&P Global Ratings Research Update (25-Jun-2026)** — provided directly by Ciarán, read in full for this update: Revised the Wagamama (Holdings) Ltd. outlook to Negative from Stable, affirming the 'B' issuer rating and 'B' rating (Recovery Rating '3', c.55%) on the £330m SSNs. Key findings integrated above (leverage trajectory, FOCF, liquidity, covenant headroom). On group structure specifically, the report is narrower than 9fin's legal analysis: it does not discuss structural subordination or ring-fencing in detail, beyond confirming (i) Wagamama is "owned by financial sponsor Apollo Global Management Inc.," (ii) the FY25 intercompany cash-sweep outflows (£14.6m) "[are] not expect[ed] to persist given the changes in the group structure post the refinancing," and (iii) FY25 revenue was depressed by the transfer of four airport sites into the TRG Concessions division prior to that division's outright sale — evidence that assets continued to move between TRG divisions after the January 2025 split and before the Concessions sale closed. S&P's governance assessment is "moderately negative," reflecting the standard view of PE-sponsor ownership prioritising shareholder returns, but does not single out the TRG service agreement as a distinct rating factor.

## Recommendation

**Add to restructuring watchlist: Yes — Monitor.**

**Rationale.** None of the three key triggers is currently active, but two are approaching and warrant active monitoring rather than dismissal:

1. **Covenant trip — Approaching, not yet active.** The RCF's 8.3x springing leverage covenant is not currently being tested (drawings are 27.6% of the facility, below the 40% activation threshold), and neither 9fin's November 2025 analysis nor S&P's June 2026 report expects an actual breach if the test is activated (S&P estimates at least 20% headroom against 8.3x). However, only c.£6.8m of further net RCF drawdown would activate testing, and management has confirmed the RCF will carry a year-end balance in FY26 due to working-capital seasonality. This is a live, quarterly-monitorable trigger, not a remote one.

2. **Leverage / maturity wall that cannot be refinanced — Not currently active.** The £330m SSNs do not mature until June 2030 (c.45 months out), well outside the standard 18-24 month screening window, so there is no near-term refinancing wall. That said, S&P's own leverage trajectory (6.9x in 2025, only falling to 6.2x-6.5x in 2026 and 5.7x-6.1x in 2027, on an IFRS16-inclusive basis) shows limited deleveraging momentum, and the negative outlook explicitly reflects the risk that leverage fails to reduce from "elevated" 2025 levels. This is one to revisit as 2028-2029 approaches and refinancing planning begins.

3. **Liquidity issue — The primary live concern.** Liquidity is currently rated "adequate" by S&P and stands at £43.7m (9fin), but this assessment is explicitly conditional on FOCF after leases improving from the -£20.8m outturn in 2025. S&P's own 2026 forecast is still negative (-£6m to -£10m), turning only marginally positive in 2027. Cash on balance sheet is thin (£3.9m as at 30-Jun-2026), so the business is structurally reliant on RCF headroom to fund working-capital swings and capex through the trading cycle. If the H2 26 EBITDA recovery that management has guided to (and that both 9fin and S&P assume in their base cases) does not materialise, liquidity is the trigger most likely to deteriorate first, and it would also pull the covenant test into range.

**Overall view.** Wagamama should stay on the watchlist as a "monitor" name rather than escalate to an active restructuring situation. The credit is showing early signs of stabilisation (positive Q3-to-date like-for-like volumes, Uber Eats delivery growth, guided EBITDA inflection in H2 26) that both 9fin and S&P's base cases rely on. The situation should be revisited each quarter against: (i) whether EBITDA growth materialises as guided in Q3/Q4 26; (ii) RCF drawings relative to the 40% covenant-activation threshold; and (iii) any further rating action, given the negative outlook from both Moody's (since Jan-2026) and S&P (since Jun-2026).

**On the TRG/Wagamama perimeter question specifically:** there is no evidence in 9fin's document set, the S&P report, or the Q2 26 bondholder transcript of a further corporate reorganisation since the January 2025 split, or of TRG/Apollo re-consolidating Wagamama's reporting into TRG. The legal Restricted Group remains confined to Wagamama. However, operational and management integration with TRG persists (shared CEO/CFO titles, ongoing TRG head-office recharges, continued review of "group central costs"), and the July 2026 Concessions sale is a further step in Apollo's stated strategy of separating TRG's businesses for individual disposal, consistent with the market's original read of the January 2025 split. Neither 9fin's legal analysis (value leakage / structural subordination risk via the uncapped TSA) nor the S&P report has been superseded or resolved by anything found in this review; both remain live structural considerations for Wagamama bondholders even though the operating credit story is separable from TRG's other divisions.
