Business Description

Amedes operates a private diagnostics platform across Germany, Austria, and Belgium, providing both routine and specialty laboratory testing. The company differentiates itself through specialty services in endocrinology, oncology, genetics, pathology, cytology, and fertility diagnostics. It covers approximately 66% of gynecologists in Germany and holds a ~20% market share in cytology. Around 55% of revenue is derived from statutory health insurance (SHI), below the German industry average of 68%, with the remainder from private-pay and other sources.

Amedes was acquired in 2021 by a consortium of OMERS Infrastructure, Goldman Sachs Asset Management, and AXA IM Alts (now BNP Paribas AM Alts) for approximately EUR 1.5bn. Previously owned by Antin Infrastructure Partners (2015-2021) and General Atlantic (2007-2015). A Luxembourg parent sits above the German operating entities, adding structural complexity.

Situation Overview

Amedes is in acute financial distress. The company has appointed Freshfields as legal advisor (March 2026) and legal and financial advisors have begun engaging with lenders and CLO investors, though no formal mandates have been agreed. Performance has materially deteriorated: H1 2025 EBITDA came in approximately 25% below plan, driven by execution issues in transformation and IT programmes and elevated exceptional costs. Overall 2025 earnings were more than 20% below budget.

Moody's forecasts an adjusted gross debt/EBITDA ratio of 11.6x for FY2025, with negative free cash flow of approximately EUR 20m and weak interest coverage. The rating agency downgraded Amedes to Caa1 in November 2025, citing a weakening financial profile, strained liquidity, and the risk that the capital structure could become unsustainable. S&P had previously downgraded the company to B- in August 2025.

While a refinancing remains technically possible, market participants note it will be difficult to fully address the company's challenges and point to a potential restructuring scenario if performance does not improve. German healthcare and laboratory regulation changes expected to be implemented from 2027 may add further headwinds through increased compliance costs.

Business Description

ERAMET is a global pure-play critical minerals producer focused on manganese, nickel, mineral sands, and lithium. The company positions itself as a strategic supplier of metals essential to the energy transition and the Western supply chain. Key assets include the world's largest manganese mine in Gabon (20+ year mine life), a major nickel operation in Indonesia (Weda Bay JV, 38.7% stake), mineral sands in Senegal, and a lithium project in Argentina (Centenario-Ratones, ramp-up phase with plans to expand to 150,000 t/a).

Revenue has contracted sharply from the 2022 peak of EUR 5.0bn to EUR 2.8bn in FY2025, driven by a protracted commodity price downcycle. Asia accounts for the largest share of revenue (~55%), followed by Europe (~25%) and North America (~15%). The company disposed of its loss-making New Caledonian nickel subsidiary SLN's operations, with EUR 320m of SLN debt neutralised via a quasi-equity conversion arranged with the French state in 2024. In 2021, ERAMET identified a potential USD 50.8m in financial fraud within its treasury operations.

Situation Overview

ERAMET is navigating a severe earnings downcycle compounded by governance disruption. Adjusted EBITDA (IFRS 16) collapsed from EUR 1.9bn in FY2022 to EUR 372m in FY2025, a decline of over 80%, driven predominantly by commodity price declines (~80% of the EBITDA drop) and weaker operational performance (~20%), particularly in manganese logistics and lithium ramp-up costs.

The prior CEO was dismissed in early 2026 for "lack of transparency and alignment" with the board. Former CEO Christel Bories has returned on an interim basis. An internal investigation has been entrusted to a new law firm (July 2026). A US-backed fund is reportedly exploring buying a stake (FT, June 2026).

In response, the board has approved a three-pillar funding plan: (1) a performance improvement programme ("Resolution") covering 50+ initiatives already underway; (2) a strategic asset review targeting sizeable monetisation in 2026; and (3) an equity-based strengthening via a planned capital increase of approximately EUR 500m, with reference shareholders committing to vote in favour at the May 2026 AGM (subsequently approved). H1 2026 results showed early improvement: sales +7.9% YoY, EBITDA +44.5% YoY, and net leverage declining by 1.0x to 4.5x.

Recommendation: ADD TO RESTRUCTURING WATCHLIST (MONITOR)
ERAMET presents a complex situation. The EUR 480m TLB matures in January 2027 (4 months), creating an imminent refinancing need. Net leverage peaked at 5.2x at year-end 2025, with interest coverage of just 0.6x. A covenant waiver was required for the December 2025 test date (gearing 125%), and a further waiver is being sought for 2026. However, the three-pillar funding plan appears to be gaining traction: the EUR ~500m capital increase was approved at the May 2026 AGM, H1 2026 results showed meaningful improvement, and the SSNs due 2028 are trading near par, suggesting the market is pricing in a successful execution. The primary risk is execution of the TLB refinancing before January 2027, the ongoing governance investigation, and the dependency on commodity price recovery for sustained deleveraging. Monitor closely; the situation could deteriorate rapidly if the capital raise stalls or commodity prices weaken further.
